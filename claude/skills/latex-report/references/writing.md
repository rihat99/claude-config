# Writing rules for reports

- **Lead with the point.** First sentence of the summary, of every section, of every paragraph.
- **One idea per paragraph**, 3–6 sentences. If a paragraph needs a "however" in the middle, split it.
- **Short sentences, plain words.** Prefer "use" to "utilize", "show" to "demonstrate", "because" to "due to the fact that".
- **Define once, then reuse.** Every symbol, acronym and dataset name is introduced the first time and never renamed. Keep one term per concept for the whole document.
- **Numbers carry units and precision that matches the measurement.** 12.3 mm, not 12.3456. Percent changes say relative to what.
- **Claims come with evidence.** A result sentence names the table or figure that shows it.
- **Say what was not done.** Limits, failed attempts and open questions get their own short section; they make the rest believable.
- **Active voice where the actor matters** ("we trained", "the model fails"), passive where it doesn't.
- **No filler**: "it is worth noting", "very", "clearly", "in order to", "various".
- **Captions are sentences** that tell the reader what to see, not what the object is.
- **Consistency of form**: same tense within a section, same decimal places within a table, same figure style across the document.
- **Read it back as the reader**: if a sentence needs a second pass, rewrite it.
